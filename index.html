Writing tips and structure
  • Keep it short. One to three sentences is 5 to 15 seconds on air. Shape: a hook, the information, then a sign-off or station name.
  • Say it the way it should sound. Write times and numbers as spoken ("nine thirty", not "9:30"), and add commas where you want a breath.
  • Give the AI facts. It only knows what you put in the brief (artist, title, time, event details) and is told not to invent the rest.
  • Delivery tags in square brackets work on Gemini speech models, for example [softly], [excited], [laughs], [short pause]. Other voices may read them aloud, so test first.
  • Tone guides the AI writer and is sent to the voice as a direction ("Say in a warm tone"). Pick a voice and model, generate, listen, and adjust: the same text can sound very different per model.
  • Sound effects like [sfx:ding] are removed on this site (the speech models cannot mix them). Use the station's Voice Lab to add them.
